Yes—flipping the polarity (+ vs. - orientation) of a DC voltage source in SPICE does the exact same thing mathematically as flipping the sign of its value.Why Flipping Polarity Works in SPICEIn circuit analysis, a voltage source with value $V$ has a potential difference of $+V$ from its + terminal to its - terminal. So:A 9 V source with + on top and - on GND puts +9 V at the top node.A 9 V source with - on top and + on GND puts -9 V at the top node—identical to a -9 V source with + on top and - on GND.Even better: that matches your actual breadboard hardware. Wiring two 9V batteries in series with the middle junction tied to 0V ground—where one battery's + terminal is the +9V rail and the other battery's - terminal is the -9V rail—is physically how a split $\pm 9\text{V}$ supply works.
